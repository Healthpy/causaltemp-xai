\section{Introduction}

A counterfactual explanation answers the question practitioners actually ask of a high-stakes sequential model: \emph{what would have had to be different in this trajectory for the prediction to have been otherwise?} \citep{wachter2017counterfactual}. In the temporal setting the question is routinely posed about clinical trajectories, industrial telemetry and financial series, and a substantial family of methods now answers it \citep{delaney2021nativeguide,ates2021comte,hollig2022tsevo,li2024mcels,paredescetina2025confetti,yan2023counts}.

The evaluation protocol those methods are judged by has been remarkably stable: \emph{validity} (does the counterfactual flip the classifier), \emph{proximity} (how far is it from the factual), \emph{sparsity} (how many entries changed), and \emph{plausibility} (does it look like training data) \citep{delaney2021nativeguide,hollig2022tsevo,pawelczyk2021carla}. Every one of those four quantities is a relation between the counterfactual and \textbf{the model}. None is a relation between the counterfactual and \textbf{the process that generated the data}. A method can therefore score perfectly on all four while proposing a trajectory the world could never have produced, or an intervention that would in fact have left the outcome untouched. The evaluation cannot tell the two apart, because it never asks the world.

This is a measurement-validity problem rather than a leaderboard problem, and it is the reason the gap has persisted. Asking whether the world agrees requires a setting where the world is known: a benchmark that ships the mechanism that generated its own data, so that the \emph{same} intervention a method claims credit for can be re-run through the true structural equations and the two outcomes compared. Real classification datasets cannot supply this. Synthetic temporal discovery benches ship a graph but no explanation method \citep{cheng2024causaltime,herdeanu2025causaldynamics,ferdous2025timegraph}. Temporal explanation benches put explainers in the loop but have no mechanism that could replay an intervention \citep{hollig2023xtscbench,nguyen2024amee}. The closest audit of third-party counterfactuals against a known SCM is tabular \citep{hollig2023semantic}.

We build that setting and use it as an instrument. Our benchmark generates multivariate time series from a known temporal structural causal model (SCM) with additive noise, which makes Pearl's abduction step an exact subtraction \citep{pearl2009causality}: the exogenous noise of a given instance is recovered rather than approximated, so the counterfactual the world would have produced \emph{for that instance} is computed exactly rather than estimated. (The invertibility of an additive-noise map is the ANM property of \citealp{hoyer2008anm}; the abduction step itself is Pearl's.) On top of it we define a \textbf{model-vs-world audit}: a proposed counterfactual is scored for mechanism consistency (CF-faith) and intervention size (do-complexity $D$), its implied intervention is realised by the true mechanism, and the disagreement between what the explanation \emph{claims} and what the world \emph{does} is decomposed additively into a trajectory term (the proposed trajectory is not the one the world would produce) and an outcome term (the classifier does not track the true label rule).

Building the instrument surfaced a construct-validity issue that we consider part of the contribution rather than an implementation detail. The audit's answer depends on how a proposal is \emph{read}: interpreting a counterfactual as a single $\dop()$ at one timestep inflates the trajectory term for densely-editing methods, while interpreting every edited slice as its own intervention drives that term to zero \emph{by construction}. Neither endpoint is privileged, so we report the quantity that is stable across both; \textbf{do-complexity} $D$, the number of timesteps a proposal must declare as interventions before the mechanism can reproduce it, alongside every disagreement number. A method with $D$ close to the trajectory length has not explained a prediction; it has rewritten the trajectory, and its agreement with the world is vacuous because nothing was left for the mechanism to predict.

A second finding is a reporting rule rather than a method comparison. Stationarity of a linear VAR requires a spectral radius below one \citep{lutkepohl2005}, so an intervention's effect on the label attenuates geometrically in the distance from the intervention time to the \emph{label site}, not to the end of the trajectory. Those two distances coincide only when the label is terminal, as it is in LinearSCM-T, NlinearSCM-T, and SpringSCM-T, and in the spring experiments of \citet{bahri2025causalfeasibility}, but not in UCR/UEA whole-series classification, where the discriminative site can be interior. Recourse feasibility is therefore a property of the benchmark configuration as much as of the method, and a validity number reported without its intervention-to-label distance is not interpretable. This is not a claim that recourse is impossible; it is a condition that every temporal recourse evaluation, including favourable ones, needs to state.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A model-vs-world audit instrument} for temporal counterfactual explanations: the proposal's own intervention is realised by the true mechanism, and the disagreement is decomposed additively into trajectory and outcome terms, each computed exactly against a known mechanism rather than estimated. Its construct validity is carried by do-complexity $D$, published beside every disagreement figure.
  \item \textbf{Intervention-to-outcome distance as a required reporting condition.} The relevant distance is $t_{\mathrm{label}}-t_0$, not $T-t_0$. The two coincide in the terminal-label families, so this contribution is the reporting rule, not an interior-label ablation.
  \item \textbf{A benchmark suite with exact structural counterfactuals}: three additive-noise temporal SCM families shipping the true graph, the true mechanism, and oracle abduction--action--prediction counterfactuals, with the label functional exposed as a configurable axis rather than fixed by construction.
  \item \textbf{An audit of published temporal CF methods} against that instrument. \todo{one sentence, once results are final.}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Scope.} Findings are established on the synthetic tier, which is the only tier possessing a ground-truth mechanism and therefore the only tier where a metric can be shown faithful to what it claims to measure. The intended families are LinearSCM-T, NlinearSCM-T, and SpringSCM-T. Headline empirical claims currently rest on LinearSCM-T and NlinearSCM-T; SpringSCM-T is specified in Appendix~\ref{app:generators} but is not yet reported at paper scale. Real-signal tiers could test whether the findings \emph{travel}, but by construction they cannot validate the metrics, and no real-signal result is used here.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Counterfactual explanation for time series.}
Temporal counterfactual methods span instance substitution (Native Guide, \citealp{delaney2021nativeguide}), segment-level substitution (CoMTE, \citealp{ates2021comte}), evolutionary search (TSEvo, \citealp{hollig2022tsevo}), saliency-guided perturbation (M-CELS, \citealp{li2024mcels}), multi-objective optimisation (CONFETTI, \citealp{paredescetina2025confetti}), symbolic abstraction (MASCOTS, \citealp{pludowski2025mascots}), and self-interpretable models that emit counterfactuals for their own predictions (CounTS, \citealp{yan2023counts}). Recent work has pushed on plausibility and temporal coherence of the edits themselves \citep{kostrzewa2026plausibility,zuin2026navigating,voets2026contex}.
What unifies the evaluation across the instance-based and search-based methods is that every reported quantity is model-relative: whether the classifier's decision changed, and how much of the input had to move for it to change. On UCR/UEA classification those methods cannot ask whether the proposed change would have worked, because the datasets do not ship a mechanism. CounTS is the exception that proves the split: it does run abduction--action--prediction, but over the latents of its own variational model, not a known data-generating process.

\paragraph{Benchmarks for temporal explanation.}
XTSC-Bench \citep{hollig2023xtscbench} and AMEE \citep{nguyen2024amee} standardise the comparison of temporal \emph{saliency} explainers (complexity, reliability, robustness, faithfulness or perturbation power), not the four CF axes above. The tabular CARLA library \citep{pawelczyk2021carla}---to be distinguished from the algorithmic-recourse framework that shares its name \citep{karimi2021recourse}---fixed a related success/proximity protocol that the temporal CF methods inherited. ExplainTS \citep{mozolewski2026explaints} states the limitation directly: with real classification tasks it has no ground-truth relevance structure, so faithfulness must be assessed by proxy. Robustness and fragility critiques of explanation methods \citep{ghorbani2019fragile,yeh2019infidelity,artelt2021robustness} and of unjustified counterfactuals \citep{laugel2019dangers,poyiadzi2020face} sharpen the model-relative axes but do not add a world-relative one. \citet{hollig2023semantic} do score nine third-party tabular CF methods against known SCMs, via relation-satisfaction rather than a Pearl trajectory, and without time.

\paragraph{Causal temporal benchmarks.}
CausalTime \citep{cheng2024causaltime}, CausalDynamics \citep{herdeanu2025causaldynamics}, TimeGraph \citep{ferdous2025timegraph} and TCS \citep{gkorgkolis2025tcs} supply time series with known \emph{lagged} causal graphs for structure recovery: the object scored is a discovered graph, never an independently produced post-hoc explanation. CauKer \citep{xie2026cauker} pretrains classification foundation models on unlabeled synthetic series from an atemporal DAG. The causal-DAG prior of \citet{orourke2026causaldag} does pair that DAG with classification labels; neither scores third-party explainers. DoFlow \citep{doflow2025} is the closest work on the counterfactual axis: a flow-based model supporting observational, interventional and counterfactual queries over a known DAG, but it is a generative forecaster in its own right, not a harness that scores third-party explainers against its ground truth. \citet{thumm2026interventional} make the relevant distinction explicit in their own appendix: their observational and interventional runs draw independent noise realisations, answering the \emph{interventional} query $P(X \mid \dop(\cdot))$ rather than the \emph{counterfactual} $P(X^{\mathrm{cf}} \mid X^{\mathrm{obs}}, \dop(\cdot))$, and they describe sharing the noise tensor---exactly the step our oracle takes---as a natural but strictly harder extension. Independently, they report the same attenuation of causal effects we characterise in Section~\ref{sec:audit}, arriving at it from a different stability mechanism (no spectral-radius constraint) and responding by restricting queries to short horizons rather than measuring the wall.

\paragraph{Causality used to constrain, versus causality used to derive.}
The causally-motivated counterfactual literature divides in a way that matters for what our benchmark can be. One branch uses a structural model to \textbf{penalise} implausible edits: the state of the art for temporal classification, \citet{bahri2025causalfeasibility}, adds a residual $\lVert T'_t - f(\Pa(T'_t))\rVert$ to a Wachter-style objective over a free perturbation $T' = T + \delta$, no $\dop()$ operator, no intervention time, no abduction. That penalty is a \emph{local, one-step} check: it asks whether each slice is close to $f$ of its own parents, independently, and never propagates the intervention forward. A counterfactual can therefore be locally mechanism-consistent at every timestep while its intervention has no effect whatsoever on the outcome, and score perfectly. The second branch \textbf{derives} counterfactuals from a causal model, but a \emph{learned} one, in order to explain that same model's own predictions: CounTS \citep{yan2023counts} performs genuine abduction--action--prediction over the latent variables of its own variational model, and the static recourse literature \citep{karimi2021recourse} derives interventions from an assumed tabular SCM. Neither branch derives the counterfactual from a \emph{known} mechanism in order to score an \emph{independently produced} explanation against it. \citet{hollig2023semantic} come closest in the static setting: they score third-party tabular CFs against known SCMs by relation satisfaction, not by replaying a Pearl trajectory. That conjunction---ground-truth rather than learned mechanism, a graph lagged over time-indexed variables rather than atemporal over whole trajectories, and the derived counterfactual used as an evaluation oracle rather than as generative machinery or a local residual---is what this work adds.

\paragraph{Causal discovery.}
DYNOTEARS \citep{pamfil2020dynotears}, PCMCI \citep{runge2019pcmci} and the broader temporal causal-inference literature \citep{runge2023causal,lippe2022citris,song2024ctrlns} address the inverse problem: recovering structure from observations. In our synthetic tiers the graph is the generator's ground truth and discovery runs only as a diagnostic, so discovery error never enters a metric; we report it as a property of the benchmark, not of any explainer. Where a graph must be discovered rather than given, discovery accuracy becomes the ceiling on everything scored against it, and we say so explicitly rather than absorbing it silently into a method's score.


\section{The Model-vs-World Audit}
\label{sec:audit}

\noindent\textbf{Measurement chain.} The phenomenon is a classifier-valid counterfactual that the data-generating process would not realise.  The task is therefore to replay the proposal's implied intervention in a known process for the same factual unit.  The mechanism-relative metrics are CF-faith and do-complexity $D$, reported for every proposal together with the disagreement $\dototal$; the supported claim is deliberately narrow: under the stated SCM and intervention reading, whether the proposal is mechanism-consistent, how large an intervention schedule it requires, and by how much the proposal and the world disagree about target-class efficacy.  This chain does not turn model validity into causal efficacy, nor synthetic agreement into a claim about an unknown real-world mechanism.

\subsection{SCM, oracle, and identifying assumptions}

An observed trajectory is $\bx=(\bx_0,\ldots,\bx_{T-1})$, with $\bx_t\in\R^k$.  Its temporal SCM has a lagged graph $G\in\{0,1\}^{k\times k\times L}$, where $G_{ij\ell}=1$ means that $X_{t-\ell}^{j}$ is a parent of $X_t^i$, and additive structural equations
\begin{equation}
  X_t^i = F_i\!\left(\Pa_G(X_t^i)\right)+\varepsilon_t^i,
  \qquad
  \Pa_G(X_t^i)=\{X_{t-\ell}^j:G_{ij\ell}=1,\ 1\leq\ell\leq L\}.
  \label{eq:temporal-scm}
\end{equation}
The binary outcome is $y_g(\bx)=\mathbb{1}\{g(\bx)>\theta\}$, where $g$ is a published label functional and $t_{\mathrm{label}}$ is the last timestep on which it depends.  We reserve $h$ for the evaluated classifier; $h$ need not implement $g$, and their disagreement is one object of the audit.

For a factual instance, additivity makes Pearl abduction an exact subtraction \citep{pearl2009causality}; the same residual is the ANM noise term \citep{hoyer2008anm}:
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_t^i
  =x_t^i-F_i\!\left(\Pa_G(x_t^i)\right).
  \label{eq:abduction}
\end{equation}
Let an intervention schedule be $I=\{(t,S_t,\mathbf{a}_t)\}$, meaning $\dop(X_t^{S_t}=\mathbf{a}_t)$.  The oracle structural counterfactual $\bx^I$ keeps the factual prefix, replaces the equations for the intervened coordinates, and recursively evaluates every remaining equation using the \emph{same} abducted noise:
\begin{equation}
  x_t^{I,i}=
  \begin{cases}
    a_t^i, & i\in S_t,\\
    F_i\!\left(\Pa_G(x_t^{I,i})\right)+\varepsilon_t^i,
      & i\notin S_t.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:oracle-cf}
\end{equation}
This shared noise tensor is what conditions the query on this factual unit; drawing fresh noise would instead estimate an interventional distribution $P(\mathbf{X}\mid\dop(I))$ \citep{pearl2009causality}.

Exactness is conditional, not universal.  It requires that the recorded $G$ and $F$ are the data-generating SCM, that causes enter with positive lag, that there is no unmeasured confounding, and that innovations enter additively and are not themselves changed by $I$.  These conditions hold by construction only in the synthetic tier.  Relaxing additivity would require an identified inverse noise model; replacing the written mechanism with a discovered one would make discovery error part of the estimand.

\subsection{CF-faith and do-complexity $D$}

Given a proposed counterfactual $\hat{\bx}$ and its first changed timestep $t_0$, CF-faith scores mechanism consistency.  It flags any retroactive edit $\hat{\bx}_{<t_0}\neq\bx_{<t_0}$ and then compares the suffix with one of two SCM continuations.  Under \texttt{noiseless\_rollout}, the reference is the deterministic skeleton $F$ with all subsequent innovations set to zero.  Under \texttt{pearl\_delta}, it is the abduction--action--prediction recursion in Eq.~\eqref{eq:oracle-cf}.  These semantics answer different questions and are reported separately: in a noisy SCM, a proposal is not expected to be both a noiseless skeleton and the unit-level Pearl counterfactual.  We never average them.  The hard score is the fraction of proposals whose suffix residual falls within a fixed tolerance; when $t_0\geq T-1$ leaves no suffix to test, the instance is unscorable rather than awarded a vacuous one.  We report CF-faith and $D$ as the two mechanism-relative metrics of a proposal.

How much of the trajectory was imposed by action is scored by $D$.  We extract, under Pearl semantics, the schedule required to reproduce $\hat{\bx}$.  At each timestep, $S_t$ contains coordinates that differ beyond tolerance from $F(\Pa_G(\hat{\bx}_t))+\beps_t$, and
\begin{equation}
  D(\hat{\bx};\bx,F)
  =\left|\{t:S_t\neq\varnothing\}\right|
  \label{eq:do-complexity}
\end{equation}
counts intervened \emph{timesteps}, not edited coordinates.  Thus $D=0$ is a no-action proposal, $D=1$ is compatible with an atomic intervention, and $D\approx T$ is a trajectory rewrite.

The need for $D$ follows from two defensible but incomplete readings.  Reading only the first changed slice as $I$ can make a dense editor look inconsistent because its later actions are omitted.  Reading every required slice as an action makes the oracle reproduce the proposal and drives $\dotraj$ to zero by construction.  We consequently retain both endpoint audits and publish $D$ beside every disagreement: neither a low $\dotraj$ nor a small $D$ alone establishes agreement.

\subsection{Model-vs-world disagreement and what ranks}

For target class $y^\star$, flip candidates are $\mathcal{C}_{y^\star}=\{n:h(\bx^{(n)})\neq y^\star\}$.  Let $I_n$ be the intervention read from an independently produced proposal $\hat{\bx}^{(n)}$, and let $\mathcal{S}_{y^\star}=\{n\in\mathcal{C}_{y^\star}:I_n\neq\varnothing\}$ be the scorable population.  We define
\begin{align}
  A &= \mathbb{E}_{n\in\mathcal{S}_{y^\star}}
       \mathbb{1}\{h(\hat{\bx}^{(n)})=y^\star\},
  &\text{(what the explanation claims)}\nonumber\\
  B &= \mathbb{E}_{n\in\mathcal{S}_{y^\star}}
       \mathbb{1}\{h(\bx^{I_n,(n)})=y^\star\},
  &\text{(the model on the world's trajectory)}\nonumber\\
  C &= \mathbb{E}_{n\in\mathcal{S}_{y^\star}}
       \mathbb{1}\{y_g(\bx^{I_n,(n)})=y^\star\},
  &\text{(what the world does).}
  \label{eq:abc}
\end{align}
No-action proposals abstain, and we report their fraction and the remaining denominator rather than count them as correct zero-effect interventions. Because this population conditions on the model being outside the target class, it is the sufficiency direction; probabilities of necessity and their PNS combination are outside this paper's scope.

The signed disagreement decomposes exactly as
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{A-C}_{\dototal}
  =\underbrace{A-B}_{\dotraj}
  +\underbrace{B-C}_{\dooutcome}.
  \label{eq:delta-decomposition}
\end{equation}
$\dotraj$ locates disagreement in the proposed versus structurally realised trajectory, whereas $\dooutcome$ locates it in the classifier versus the published label rule.  A positive $\dototal$ is over-claimed efficacy; a negative value means that the classifier misses an effect present under the label rule.  Distance from zero measures disagreement, while the sign is retained for diagnosis.

We report CF-faith, $D$, and $\dototal$ jointly for every method; we do not combine them into a scalar.  Progress means mechanism-consistent proposals, small intervention schedules, and small model--world disagreement. $\dotraj$ and $\dooutcome$ explain the source of the gap; neither is a second leaderboard, and $\dotraj$ is never interpreted without the intervention reading and $D$.

\subsection{The intervention-to-label reporting rule}

Let $\boldsymbol{\delta}_t=\bx_t^I-\bx_t$ denote the propagated effect of an atomic intervention at $t_0$.  In the linear $L=1$ tier, after that action it obeys $\boldsymbol{\delta}_t=A\boldsymbol{\delta}_{t-1}$.  Stationarity of a VAR requires $\rho(A)<1$ \citep{lutkepohl2005}.  Gelfand's formula then implies that, for any $r\in(\rho(A),1)$, there is a finite constant $K$ such that \citep{hornjohnson2013}
\begin{equation}
  \|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{t_{\mathrm{label}}}\|
  \leq K r^{\,t_{\mathrm{label}}-t_0}
      \|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{t_0}\|.
  \label{eq:horizon-decay}
\end{equation}
Contractive nonlinear transitions give the analogous product of local Lipschitz factors.  Thus recourse feasibility depends on the benchmark configuration and on $d_{\mathrm{label}}=t_{\mathrm{label}}-t_0$, not merely on the method.  If $t_0>t_{\mathrm{label}}$, the intervention cannot affect the label at all; if the label is terminal, $T-1-t_0$ happens to equal the relevant distance but does not define it.

Every validity and model-vs-world result must therefore report the label functional, $t_{\mathrm{label}}$, $t_0$ (or its empirical distribution), and $t_{\mathrm{label}}-t_0$; multi-action proposals additionally require $D$. We do not compare configurations at different label distances as though they posed the same recourse task.  A classifier flip beyond the mechanism's effective horizon remains a model-relative fact; without this distance it is not evidence that the proposed intervention could have changed the world.
\section{Benchmark, methods, and controls}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Generators as challenge factors}
\label{sec:setup-generators}

We organise the benchmark around factors that can change the difficulty or meaning of a temporal intervention, rather than around a catalogue of datasets.  The first factor is the \emph{mechanism family}.  LinearSCM-T is a stable VAR; NlinearSCM-T replaces its linear transition with masked per-node MLPs; and SpringSCM-T uses an undamped second-order spring system, with positions and velocities as observed channels.  All three retain additive innovations.  This restriction is load-bearing: it preserves the exact unit-level abduction in Eq.~\eqref{eq:abduction}, and hence keeps the audit identical across families.

The second factor is \emph{contraction}.  LinearSCM-T is contractive by construction ($\rho\le 0.9$).  NlinearSCM-T is bounded ($\mathrm{gain}\cdot\tanh$, $\mathrm{decay}<1$) and empirically dissipative; the shipped spectral-norm cap of $6.0$ does not itself imply branch-level contraction.  SpringSCM-T is undamped and near-conservative.  Family names are not treated as evidence of the dynamics: empirical contraction characterises the mechanism, and is not a per-dataset pipeline output.  This contrast tests whether the horizon effect in Eq.~\eqref{eq:horizon-decay} follows dissipation rather than a particular transition parameterisation.  The third factor is \emph{temporal placement}: trajectory length $T$, intervention time $t_0$, and label site $t_{\mathrm{label}}$.  All three families use a terminal threshold label, so $t_{\mathrm{label}}-t_0$ and $T-1-t_0$ coincide.  Default CF generation places $t_0/T\in\{0.25,0.5\}$ (i.e.\ $t_0\in\{25,50\}$ at $T=100$).  Horizon sweeps convert those fractions to absolute $T-t_0$ so the operating point can be located on the decay curve rather than silently changing with $T$.

The paper reports three families: LinearSCM-T, NlinearSCM-T, and SpringSCM-T.  All three use $k=10$, $L=1$, $T=100$, $N=10{,}000$, a terminal label, and Laplace innovations.  LinearSCM-T and NlinearSCM-T use graph density $0.2$; SpringSCM-T uses particle-level coupling probability $0.3$.  Generator cards are given in Appendix~\ref{app:generators}.  The metric taxonomy is given in Appendix~\ref{app:metrics}.  Results report the method-level conventional and mechanism-relative quantities; dataset construction checks remain outside the manuscript tables.

\subsection{Tiers and what each can support}
\label{sec:setup-tiers}

The tiers separate internal from external validity; they are not a realism ladder on which later tiers inherit stronger causal claims.  \textbf{Tier~1} contains the three synthetic SCM families, with the written $G$, $F$, noise, and label functional.  It is the only tier in which the oracle trajectory is known, and therefore the only tier used to validate CF-faith, $D$, or the model--world decomposition.  The reported causal audit covers LinearSCM-T, NlinearSCM-T, and SpringSCM-T.

\textbf{Tier~2} would ask whether the intervention-to-label dependence observed in Tier~1 appears on real multivariate classification signals that have no ground-truth graph.  It cannot validate a causal metric.  No Tier~2 or Tier~3 result is used in this paper.

\subsection{Methods and controls}
\label{sec:setup-methods}

The predictive model is a fixed LSTM.  The baselines are Wachter \citep{wachter2017counterfactual}, COMTE \citep{ates2021comte}, CONFETTI \citep{paredescetina2025confetti}, and M-CELS \citep{li2024mcels}; none receives the true graph or mechanism.  The controls are PearlSCMRecourse and NoiselessSCMRecourse (Appendix~\ref{app:controls}).  TSCausalCF \citep{bahri2025causalfeasibility} receives the true graph and mechanism and is evaluated as a residual-penalty foil.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Conventional evaluation gives a reassuring but incomplete account of the
proposals.  Across the three mechanism families, the standard explainers
usually achieve nontrivial classifier validity, frequently retain that
validity under shift, and exhibit familiar trade-offs between validity,
proximity, and sparsity.  The model--world audit changes this conclusion:
hard rollout CF-faith is zero in every standard method--family comparison,
Pearl CF-faith is absent except for subsets of M-CELS proposals, and three of
the four explainers require actions at essentially the complete trajectory.
Most importantly, the target-class rate claimed by the proposal exceeds the
rate realised by the structural mechanism in every comparison.

\label{sec:res-conventional}

ground-truth mechanism for all seven evaluated methods.  The four standard

controls in each family.  Among the standard explainers, validity is at least
$0.50$ in $11$ of the $12$ standard method--family cells and at least $0.80$

\subsection{Controls validate the two intervention semantics}
\label{sec:res-controls}

The control rows in Tables~\ref{tab:conventional-results}
and~\ref{tab:causal-audit-results} first establish that the audit distinguishes
its intended objects.  NoiselessSCMRecourse has hard rollout CF-faith $1$ and Pearl
CF-faith $0$ in every family; PearlSCMRecourse has the mirror image.  Under
Pearl semantics, PearlSCMRecourse requires one intervention timestep in every
family and has $\dototal=0.29$, $0.00$, and $0.02$ on LinearSCM-T,
NlinearSCM-T, and SpringSCM-T.  NoiselessSCMRecourse is generated from one
noiseless action, but reproducing its noise-deleting continuation under Pearl
semantics requires $D=75.0$, $59.7$, and $73.0$.  The controls therefore
recover the semantics fixed by their construction and show why CF-faith and
$D$ must name the intervention reading.

\subsection{SCM regularisation does not close the gap}
\label{sec:res-foil}

TSCausalCF is evaluated separately because it receives the true graph and
mechanism but uses them as a local residual penalty rather than an explicit
abduction--action--prediction operator.  It requires $D=100$ in all three
families and never attains hard Pearl CF-faith.  On LinearSCM-T it flips every
selected instance and attains rollout CF-faith $0.55$, yet the world-replayed
target rate is $0$, giving $\dototal=1.00$.  Local slice-wise consistency is
therefore insufficient to establish that an implied intervention propagates
to the outcome.

The failure changes form on the other mechanisms.  TSCausalCF finds no
classifier-flipping proposal on NlinearSCM-T and succeeds on only $5\%$ of the
SpringSCM-T cases.  On NlinearSCM-T, $\dototal=-0.36$: the proposals do not
reach the target class, while the world replay does so in $36\%$ of cases.  On
SpringSCM-T, both rates are near zero and $\dototal=0.01$.  The sign separates
under-claiming from over-claiming, but neither result closes the audit gap:
the foil still declares the complete trajectory as action.  This comparison
isolates residual regularisation from explicit structural derivation; it does
not test graph estimation.

\subsection{Intervention timing and audit populations qualify comparisons}
\label{sec:res-horizon}

All three families place the label at $t_{\mathrm{label}}=99$, but the methods
do not pose the same temporal intervention problem.  COMTE and CONFETTI begin
every nonempty proposal at $t_0=0$, as does TSCausalCF.  Wachter also begins at
$0$ except for a small number of LinearSCM-T proposals beginning as late as
$t_0=18$.  Their actions therefore sit $81$--$99$ steps from the label.  COMTE
nevertheless reports validity $1.00$ in all three families, while the
world-replayed rates are only $0.00$, $0.16$, and $0.03$.  The classifier flip
is a model-relative success, but it does not show that the implied early
intervention would produce the target outcome.

M-CELS occupies a different temporal regime.  Among proposals with nonempty
schedules, its median label distance is $6$ steps on LinearSCM-T, $67.5$ on
NlinearSCM-T, and $95$ on SpringSCM-T.  These cross-method differences are
qualifications, not evidence that label distance alone causes the performance
gap.  Every result must therefore retain the label functional,
$t_{\mathrm{label}}$, the empirical $t_0$ distribution,
$t_{\mathrm{label}}-t_0$, and, for multi-action proposals, $D$.

\section{Metric definitions and axis taxonomy}
\label{app:metrics}

The validity argument is in Section~\ref{sec:audit}.  This appendix records membership, populations, and the numerical conventions needed to reproduce a table.  Every reported quantity belongs to exactly one axis.  Axis~A scores the \emph{benchmark} (one number per generated dataset).  Axes~B and~C score \emph{methods}.  Presenting all three as parallel per-method columns is a category error.  A former concept-quality axis was deleted; there is no Axis~D.  The audit quantities $A$, $B$, $C$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:abc} are not Axis~A/B/C.

\paragraph{Changed-element predicate.}
A coordinate is changed when $\lvert\hat{x}_t^i-x_t^i\rvert>10^{-3}$.  That single threshold defines $t_0$ (the first changed timestep), CF-faith's retroactive gate, sparsity, and the action set $S_t$ inside $D$.  If no coordinate exceeds it, $t_0=T-1$ and the instance is CF-faith-degenerate.

\paragraph{Axis C, model-relative.}
Validity is the fraction of proposals with $h(\hat{\bx})=y^\star$.  It is reported with $t_{\mathrm{label}}$, $t_0$, and $t_{\mathrm{label}}-t_0$; without that distance it is not interpretable (Section~\ref{sec:audit}).  Proximity is $\lVert\hat{\bx}-\bx\rVert_p$ over the flattened $T\times k$ trajectory, $p\in\{1,2\}$.  Sparsity is the fraction of the $T\times k$ entries left \emph{unchanged} ($\lvert\Delta\rvert\le 10^{-3}$).  Higher means a sparser edit; $1$ means the proposal equals the factual.  This is the complement of an $L_0$ altered-fraction; we emit that complement as \texttt{frac\_altered} and do not treat the two as interchangeable.  Channel and timepoint variants are the fractions of channels (resp.\ timesteps) left entirely untouched.  OOD plausibility is the Isolation Forest decision score on the flattened trajectory (higher $=$ more in-distribution).  TRSI is implemented as a mechanism-free smoothness descriptor and is not reported here.

\paragraph{CF-faith, both semantics.}
Given $\hat{\bx}$ and $t_0$, CF-faith fails if any prefix coordinate is changed (retroactive edit).  Otherwise it compares the suffix with one of two continuations of $F$, never averaged.  Under \texttt{noiseless\_rollout} the reference is the deterministic skeleton (subsequent innovations set to zero).  Under \texttt{pearl\_delta} it is the abduction--action--prediction recursion of Eq.~\eqref{eq:oracle-cf}.  A proposal in a noisy SCM is not expected to be both.  The residual is the mean $L_1$ distance between the proposed suffix and the reference; the hard score is $1$ if that residual is below $10^{-3}$ and $0$ otherwise.  The soft score $\exp(-\mathrm{residual})$ is the graded companion.  This paper reports the hard fraction.  When $t_0\ge T-1$ both scores are NaN, not $1$: there is no suffix to test.  Aggregates use the scorable subset and are published with \texttt{frac\_degenerate} and \texttt{n\_cf\_faith\_scorable}.

\paragraph{Do-complexity and populations.}
$D$ is Eq.~\eqref{eq:do-complexity}, counted on timesteps, under Pearl semantics unless a noiseless reading is named.  $D=0$ is a no-action proposal; \texttt{frac\_vacuous} is that row of the same distribution.  Flip candidates are $\mathcal{C}_{y^\star}=\{n:h(\bx^{(n)})\neq y^\star\}$.  The scorable population $\mathcal{S}_{y^\star}$ further drops empty schedules.  No-action proposals abstain from Eq.~\eqref{eq:abc}; we report their fraction and the remaining denominator rather than scoring them as correct zero-effect interventions.  Because $\mathcal{S}_{y^\star}$ conditions on the model being outside the target class, the estimand is the sufficiency direction.  PN, PS, and their PNS combination are defined in the protocol and are outside this paper's scope.

\paragraph{Sign of $\Delta$.}
$\dototal=A-C$ is signed.  Positive means over-claimed efficacy (the explanation asserts a target-class outcome the world denies); negative means the classifier misses an effect present under the label rule $y_g$.  Distance from zero measures disagreement; the sign is retained for diagnosis.  $\dotraj$ and $\dooutcome$ locate the source and are not a second leaderboard.  A $\Delta$ of either kind is never cited without $D$ and the intervention reading.

\paragraph{Axis B.}
Shift-VR is validity retention on a held-out environment of the same SCM (frozen classifier): $\mathrm{validity}_{\mathrm{shift}}/\mathrm{validity}_{\mathrm{base}}$ when base validity is at least $0.30$.  It is reported in Table~\ref{tab:conventional-results}; below that threshold, the base and shifted rates are shown but the unstable ratio is suppressed.  Input sensitivity is a Lipschitz constant of the explanation in the input, where exposed, and is not reported.

\paragraph{Axis A.}
Computed once per dataset against the written graph, not against any explainer.  SHD is the number of disagreeing lagged-edge decisions.  Lag accuracy is recall of true lagged edges; lagged-edge F1 is the precision-aware companion.  Graph AUC is edge-level ROC against ground truth (NaN if the score matrix is constant or the labels are degenerate).  Residual dependence is the mean $\lvert$Pearson $r\rvert$ of mechanism residuals on non-adjacent channels.  Graph-error is the signed difference of CF-faith against the true graph minus CF-faith against an inferred graph; it can be negative and is not scale-free across mechanism families.  These construction checks are retained in the experiment artifacts but are not manuscript results.  On SpringSCM-T the recorded graph omits the position-from-velocity self-coupling (Appendix~\ref{app:generators}), so those Axis~A numbers are not comparable to the VAR/MLP scores without that note.

\section{Generator specifications}
\label{app:generators}

The paper reports three families: LinearSCM-T, NlinearSCM-T, and SpringSCM-T.  Other generator scales exist in the code for plumbing and tests and are not reported.

All three families share the same outer protocol.  A lagged graph $G\in\{0,1\}^{k\times k\times L}$ is drawn with independent Bernoulli($s$) edges, except that lag-1 self-loops are forbidden.  Innovations are additive Laplace with scale $0.1$.  A burn-in of $100$ steps is discarded.  The binary label is a median split, over the generated population, of channel~$0$ at $t_{\mathrm{label}}=T-1$.  The threshold itself is not persisted.  Observation is the identity: nonlinear mixing $x=g(z)$ is out of scope, because it would make abduction partial.

\paragraph{LinearSCM-T.}
A stable VAR,
\begin{equation}
  x_t=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}A_\ell x_{t-\ell}+\varepsilon_t.
\end{equation}
Entries of each $A_\ell$ are drawn from $U(-0.5,0.5)$, masked by $G$, and rescaled so that $\rho(A_\ell)\le 0.9$.  With $L=1$ this is the contraction that Eq.~\eqref{eq:horizon-decay} uses.

\paragraph{NlinearSCM-T.}
The same $G$ and additive noise, with the linear map replaced by a per-node two-layer MLP
\begin{equation}
  x_t^i=\mathrm{decay}_i\,x_{t-1}^i+\mathrm{gain}\cdot\tanh\bigl(\mathrm{MLP}_i(\text{masked lags})\bigr)+\varepsilon_t^i.
\end{equation}
Registered hyperparameters are hidden width $16$, $\mathrm{gain}=1.5$, $\mathrm{decay}_i\sim U[0.1,0.4]$, spectral-norm cap $6.0$ on each weight matrix, and init-gain $3.0$.  Masked inputs are scaled by $1/\sqrt{\max(n_{\mathrm{parents}},1)}$.  Boundedness follows from $\mathrm{gain}\cdot\tanh$ and $\mathrm{decay}<1$; the cap of $6.0$ does not itself make the nonlinear branch Lipschitz-contractive, so dissipation is an empirical property of the family rather than a constructive one.  A randomly initialised two-layer net stays close to its own linearisation, so this family is a nonlinear \emph{parameterisation} of an additive-noise transition, not a strongly curved mechanism.

\paragraph{SpringSCM-T.}
An undamped second-order particle system, adopted as used by \citet{bahri2025causalfeasibility}.  Five particles are exposed as $k=10$ channels (position then velocity).  One symplectic-Euler step with spring constant $0.3$, $\Delta t=0.1$, and unit mass gives
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    a_t^i &= -\sum_j K_{ij}(x_t^i-x_t^j),\\
    v_{t+1}^i &= v_t^i+\Delta t\,a_t^i,\\
    x_{t+1}^i &= x_t^i+\Delta t\,v_t^i.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Additive innovations are then applied as in the other families.  $K$ is $0.3$ times a particle-level Bernoulli($0.3$) mask with no self-coupling; the last two particles have their incoming edges zeroed, so two exogenous particles exist by construction.  There is no damping term.  The recorded graph stores only the velocity-from-position couplings; the position-from-velocity self-coupling is treated as baseline structure and omitted, so Axis~A graph scores on this family are not comparable to the VAR/MLP scores without that note.  The label is the same channel-$0$ median threshold as the other families (first-particle position), not an energy functional.

\paragraph{Registered deviations.}
The original design space listed $k\in\{5,10,20\}$, $L\in\{1,3\}$, $s\in\{0.1,0.2,0.3\}$, $T\in\{50,100,200\}$, and a segment count.  The three families lock $k=10$, $L=1$, $T=100$, $N=10{,}000$. \section{Control methods}
\label{app:controls}

PearlSCMRecourse and NoiselessSCMRecourse are positive controls constructed by this work, not adapters of a published explainer.  Both receive the true mechanism $F$.  Both leave the factual prefix unchanged, optimise a single slice $\bx_{t_0}+\boldsymbol{\delta}$, and let $F$ produce the suffix.  A non-vacuous proposal therefore has $D=1$ by construction.  Candidate intervention times are $t_0/T\in\{0.25,0.5\}$.  The search minimises
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_{\mathrm{pred}}\,\mathrm{CE}\bigl(h(\hat{\bx}),\,y^\star\bigr)
  +\lambda_{\mathrm{prox}}\lVert\boldsymbol{\delta}\rVert_2^2
\end{equation}
with Adam over $\boldsymbol{\delta}\in\R^k$ (300 steps for NoiselessSCMRecourse, 500 for PearlSCMRecourse).  If that search does not produce a genuine above-tolerance flip, one prediction-only retry with $\lambda_{\mathrm{prox}}=0$ is allowed.  Registered weights are $\lambda_{\mathrm{pred}}=1$ for both controls, $\lambda_{\mathrm{prox}}=0.1$ for PearlSCMRecourse and $\lambda_{\mathrm{prox}}=0.5$ for NoiselessSCMRecourse.

\paragraph{PearlSCMRecourse.}
The suffix is the abduction--action--prediction recursion of Eq.~\eqref{eq:oracle-cf}:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{x}_t
  =
  \begin{cases}
    x_t, & t<t_0,\\
    x_{t_0}+\boldsymbol{\delta}, & t=t_0,\\
    F\bigl(\Pa_G(\hat{\bx}_t)\bigr)+\varepsilon_t, & t>t_0,
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $\varepsilon_t$ abducted from the factual instance as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:abduction}.  Hard CF-faith under \texttt{pearl\_delta} is therefore $1$ by construction, provided the instance is scorable ($t_0<T-1$).

\paragraph{NoiselessSCMRecourse.}
The same search, with all subsequent innovations set to zero:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{x}_t
  =
  \begin{cases}
    x_t, & t<t_0,\\
    x_{t_0}+\boldsymbol{\delta}, & t=t_0,\\
    F\bigl(\Pa_G(\hat{\bx}_t)\bigr), & t>t_0.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Hard CF-faith under \texttt{noiseless\_rollout} is $1$ by construction on scorable instances.  The two controls are not interchangeable: a noiseless skeleton is not the unit-level Pearl counterfactual, and we never average the two CF-faith semantics.
