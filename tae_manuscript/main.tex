\begin{abstract}
Temporal counterfactual explanations are usually evaluated by whether they
flip a classifier and remain close, sparse, or plausible relative to the
observed input.  These criteria do not establish whether the data-generating
process would realise the proposed trajectory or its claimed outcome.  We
introduce a model--world audit built on additive-noise temporal structural
causal models, where unit-level abduction is exact.  The audit separately
measures mechanism consistency (CF-faith), the number of timesteps that must
be treated as interventions (do-complexity $D$), and the disagreement between
the proposed and structurally replayed outcomes through an additive $A/B/C$
decomposition.  We apply it to four temporal counterfactual baselines, a
causal-residual foil, and two structural controls on three synthetic mechanism
families.  Conventional metrics frequently give a reassuring account, yet
hard rollout CF-faith is zero in every baseline--family comparison, Pearl
CF-faith is nonzero only for subsets of M-CELS proposals, and the claimed
target-class rate exceeds the structurally realised rate in every baseline
comparison.  The observed disagreement is predominantly trajectory-level,
and three baselines require near-complete intervention schedules.  These
results demonstrate a measurement gap in the reported synthetic benchmark
realizations; they do not establish stable method rankings or transfer to
unknown real-world mechanisms.
\end{abstract}


\section{Introduction}

A temporal counterfactual explains what would have had to change in a trajectory for a classifier to decide otherwise \citep{wachter2017counterfactual}.  Existing methods answer this question through substitution, gradient search, evolutionary search, or learned latent models \citep{delaney2021nativeguide,ates2021comte,hollig2022tsevo,li2024mcels,paredescetina2025confetti,yan2023counts}.

They are usually judged by classifier validity, proximity, sparsity, and plausibility \citep{delaney2021nativeguide,hollig2022tsevo,pawelczyk2021carla}.  These quantities relate a proposal to the model or observed data, not to the process that generated the trajectory.  They can therefore reward a classifier flip even when the proposed trajectory is structurally impossible or its implied intervention would not produce the claimed outcome.

This is a measurement-validity problem, not a leaderboard problem.  Asking whether the world agrees requires a benchmark that exposes the mechanism that generated its data, so the proposal's implied intervention can be replayed through the true structural equations.  Real classification datasets cannot supply this; temporal discovery benchmarks expose graphs but do not audit explanations, while explanation benchmarks lack a replayable mechanism \citep{cheng2024causaltime,herdeanu2025causaldynamics,ferdous2025timegraph,hollig2023xtscbench,nguyen2024amee}.

We build that instrument from known additive-noise temporal SCMs.  Additivity makes Pearl abduction an exact subtraction \citep{pearl2009causality,hoyer2008anm}, so the unit-level structural counterfactual is computed rather than estimated.  The audit asks three distinct questions: does the proposal follow the mechanism (CF-faith), how many timesteps must be declared actions before the mechanism can reproduce it (do-complexity $D$), and does structural replay preserve the proposal's claimed target-class outcome (the $A/B/C$ disagreement decomposition)?

The intervention reading is load-bearing.  Reading only the first changed slice can penalise later edits that were intended as actions; reading every required slice as an action reproduces any proposal by construction.  We therefore report $D$ beside every disagreement.  A proposal with $D\approx T$ is a trajectory rewrite under this audit, because little remains for the mechanism to predict.

Temporal placement is equally important.  In a contractive process, an intervention's effect attenuates with its distance to the label site, $t_{\mathrm{label}}-t_0$, not automatically with its distance to the trajectory end \citep{lutkepohl2005}.  Every validity or replay result must therefore identify the label functional, intervention time, and label distance.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item An exact \textbf{model--world audit} that reports CF-faith, $D$, and an additive trajectory/outcome disagreement decomposition without combining them into a scalar.
  \item A \textbf{temporal reporting contract}: every result identifies $t_{\mathrm{label}}-t_0$, the intervention reading, and the scorable population.
  \item A \textbf{controlled demonstration} on three additive-noise SCM families: conventional metrics remain favourable while replay reveals low mechanism consistency, large schedules, and over-claimed target-class efficacy.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Scope.} We report descriptive point estimates for LinearSCM-T, NlinearSCM-T, and SpringSCM-T.  They show that conventional and mechanism-relative assessments can diverge; they do not establish stable method rankings or transfer to unknown real-world mechanisms.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Counterfactual explanation for time series.}
Methods span instance or segment substitution, saliency-guided optimisation, evolutionary search, and learned latent models \citep{delaney2021nativeguide,ates2021comte,hollig2022tsevo,li2024mcels,paredescetina2025confetti,yan2023counts}.  Work on plausibility and temporal coherence improves the proposal relative to observed data \citep{kostrzewa2026plausibility,zuin2026navigating,voets2026contex}, but standard real-data benchmarks still cannot replay it through a known mechanism.  CounTS performs abduction--action--prediction in its learned latent model; our question instead uses the known data-generating process to audit an independently produced explanation.
This distinction is not semantic: proximity asks whether the edit is small,
plausibility whether it resembles observed data, and validity whether a model
changes its decision.  None asks whether the proposed intervention propagates
through the process to the target label.  A causally impossible trajectory can
therefore remain conventionally successful.

\paragraph{Benchmarks for temporal explanation.}
XTSC-Bench and AMEE standardise temporal saliency evaluation, while CARLA systematises model-relative counterfactual success and distance \citep{hollig2023xtscbench,nguyen2024amee,pawelczyk2021carla}.  Robustness and unjustified-counterfactual critiques sharpen those axes but do not add a world-relative replay \citep{ghorbani2019fragile,yeh2019infidelity,laugel2019dangers,poyiadzi2020face}.  The closest audit of third-party explanations against known SCMs is static and relation-based rather than a Pearl trajectory \citep{hollig2023semantic}.
ExplainTS makes the real-data limitation explicit: without known relevance or
mechanism, faithfulness is necessarily proxy-based \citep{mozolewski2026explaints}.
Our benchmark complements rather than replaces such suites.  Their axes remain
necessary for model-relative quality; the replay adds a missing estimand.

\paragraph{Causal temporal benchmarks.}
CausalTime, CausalDynamics, TimeGraph, and TCS expose lagged graphs to score structure recovery, not post-hoc explanations \citep{cheng2024causaltime,herdeanu2025causaldynamics,ferdous2025timegraph,gkorgkolis2025tcs}.  DoFlow supports causal queries as a generative forecaster rather than an external audit \citep{doflow2025}.  The distinction is unit-level: drawing fresh noise estimates an interventional distribution, whereas retaining the abducted factual noise answers the counterfactual query \citep{thumm2026interventional,pearl2009causality}.
This is why a known graph alone is insufficient.  The audit also needs the
transition functions, the factual innovations, and an exposed label rule so
that the same proposed action can be evaluated by both classifier and world.

\paragraph{Causality used to constrain, versus causality used to derive.}
TSCausalCF constrains a freely edited trajectory with local structural residuals \citep{bahri2025causalfeasibility}; CounTS and causal recourse derive counterfactuals from learned or assumed models \citep{yan2023counts,karimi2021recourse}.  Our audit occupies a different role: the true temporal mechanism is an evaluation oracle for proposals generated independently of it.  The TSCausalCF foil tests whether access to that mechanism as a residual penalty is equivalent to explicit abduction--action--prediction.
A local residual can be small at each edited slice without representing the
trajectory produced by one intervention.  Conversely, a structurally derived
counterfactual is faithful to the model that generated it but cannot validate
that learned model.  The known-SCM audit separates these two uses of causality.

\paragraph{Causal discovery.}
Causal discovery solves the inverse problem of recovering structure \citep{pamfil2020dynotears,runge2019pcmci,runge2023causal}.  Here the generator's graph is ground truth and discovery checks remain outside the manuscript results.  With an estimated graph, recovery error would become part of the audit rather than a hidden property of the explainer.


\section{The Model-vs-World Audit}
\label{sec:audit}

The audit replays a proposal's implied intervention in the known process for the same factual unit.  It reports mechanism consistency, intervention size, and claimed-versus-realised target-class efficacy under a named intervention reading; it does not turn classifier validity into causal efficacy.

\subsection{SCM, oracle, and identifying assumptions}

For $\bx=(\bx_0,\ldots,\bx_{T-1})$, $\bx_t\in\R^k$, the lagged SCM is
\begin{equation}
  X_t^i = F_i\!\left(\Pa_G(X_t^i)\right)+\varepsilon_t^i,
  \qquad
  \Pa_G(X_t^i)=\{X_{t-\ell}^j:G_{ij\ell}=1,\ 1\leq\ell\leq L\}.
  \label{eq:temporal-scm}
\end{equation}
The world label is $y_g(\bx)=\mathbb{1}\{g(\bx)>\theta\}$, whose final dependency is $t_{\mathrm{label}}$; $h$ denotes the evaluated classifier.

Additivity makes Pearl abduction exact \citep{pearl2009causality,hoyer2008anm}:
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_t^i
  =x_t^i-F_i\!\left(\Pa_G(x_t^i)\right).
  \label{eq:abduction}
\end{equation}
For schedule $I=\{(t,S_t,\mathbf{a}_t)\}$, the oracle keeps the factual prefix, replaces intervened equations, and recursively evaluates the rest with the same abducted noise:
\begin{equation}
  x_t^{I,i}=
  \begin{cases}
    a_t^i, & i\in S_t,\\
    F_i\!\left(\Pa_G(x_t^{I,i})\right)+\varepsilon_t^i,
      & i\notin S_t.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:oracle-cf}
\end{equation}
Fresh noise would instead answer an interventional query.  Exactness requires the recorded $G,F$ to be the data-generating SCM, positive lags, no unmeasured confounding, and additive innovations unchanged by $I$; Appendix~\ref{app:audit-details} records the derivation and claim boundary.
The shared innovations are essential: they condition the replay on the same
factual unit rather than comparing the proposal with another random trajectory.
The oracle is therefore exact only inside the benchmark, where these
identifying assumptions hold by construction.

\subsection{CF-faith and do-complexity $D$}

Given proposal $\hat{\bx}$ and first change $t_0$, CF-faith rejects retroactive edits and compares the suffix with either a zero-innovation deterministic rollout or the unit-level Pearl recursion in Eq.~\eqref{eq:oracle-cf}.  These semantics answer different questions and are never averaged.  Hard CF-faith is the fraction within tolerance; $t_0\geq T-1$ is unscorable because no suffix remains.
The noiseless score asks whether the proposal follows the deterministic
skeleton after its first action; the Pearl score asks whether it follows the
unit-level continuation with factual innovations retained.  A noisy proposal
need not satisfy both.  Publishing the scorable denominator prevents a
terminal or empty edit from earning vacuous faithfulness.

Under Pearl semantics, $D$ counts timesteps that must be imposed to reproduce $\hat{\bx}$:
\begin{equation}
  D(\hat{\bx};\bx,F)
  =\left|\{t:S_t\neq\varnothing\}\right|
  \label{eq:do-complexity}
\end{equation}
It counts timesteps, not coordinates: $D=0$ is no action, $D=1$ is compatible with an atomic intervention, and $D\approx T$ is a trajectory rewrite.  Reading only the first slice can omit intended later actions; reading the complete schedule drives $\dotraj$ to zero by construction.  We therefore publish $D$ beside every disagreement.
These endpoint readings expose complementary failures.  A large first-slice
trajectory error may mean that later edits were omitted from the action; a
perfect full-schedule replay may mean that the method declared the whole
trajectory.  Neither low disagreement nor low $D$ alone establishes a useful
counterfactual.

\subsection{Model-vs-world disagreement and its decomposition}

For target class $y^\star$, flip candidates are $\mathcal{C}_{y^\star}=\{n:h(\bx^{(n)})\neq y^\star\}$.  Let $I_n$ be the intervention read from an independently produced proposal $\hat{\bx}^{(n)}$, and let $\mathcal{S}_{y^\star}=\{n\in\mathcal{C}_{y^\star}:I_n\neq\varnothing\}$ be the scorable population.  We define
\begin{align}
  A &= \mathbb{E}_{n\in\mathcal{S}_{y^\star}}
       \mathbb{1}\{h(\hat{\bx}^{(n)})=y^\star\},
  &\text{(what the explanation claims)}\nonumber\\
  B &= \mathbb{E}_{n\in\mathcal{S}_{y^\star}}
       \mathbb{1}\{h(\bx^{I_n,(n)})=y^\star\},
  &\text{(the model on the world's trajectory)}\nonumber\\
  C &= \mathbb{E}_{n\in\mathcal{S}_{y^\star}}
       \mathbb{1}\{y_g(\bx^{I_n,(n)})=y^\star\},
  &\text{(what the world does).}
  \label{eq:abc}
\end{align}
No-action proposals abstain; we report their fraction and the remaining denominator.  This target-class population estimates the sufficiency direction; PN and combined PNS are out of scope.

The signed disagreement decomposes exactly as
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{A-C}_{\dototal}
  =\underbrace{A-B}_{\dotraj}
  +\underbrace{B-C}_{\dooutcome}.
  \label{eq:delta-decomposition}
\end{equation}
$\dotraj$ locates proposal-versus-replay disagreement; $\dooutcome$ locates classifier-versus-label-rule disagreement on the replay.  Positive $\dototal$ is over-claimed efficacy.  We report CF-faith, $D$, and the signed decomposition jointly, never as a scalar leaderboard.
The intermediate rate $B$ is what makes the diagnosis possible: $A-B$ changes
only the trajectory presented to the fixed classifier, whereas $B-C$ changes
only the decision rule applied to the replayed trajectory.  The decomposition
therefore distinguishes an implausible proposal from classifier--world
misalignment without treating either term as a new ranking axis.

\subsection{The intervention-to-label reporting rule}

For a stable linear process, an atomic intervention's effect is bounded by a geometric factor in $t_{\mathrm{label}}-t_0$; Appendix~\ref{app:audit-details} gives the derivation.  Every validity and replay result must therefore report the label functional, $t_{\mathrm{label}}$, empirical $t_0$, and label distance; multi-action proposals also require $D$.  A classifier flip without this distance is not evidence that the intervention could change the world.
The distance happens to equal $T-1-t_0$ for the terminal labels reported here,
but trajectory end does not define it.  Methods acting at different times pose
different recourse problems even on the same factual population.
\section{Benchmark, methods, and controls}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Generators as challenge factors}
\label{sec:setup-generators}

We report three additive-noise families: a contractive VAR (LinearSCM-T), a bounded masked-MLP transition (NlinearSCM-T), and an undamped second-order particle system (SpringSCM-T).  All use $k=10$, $L=1$, $T=100$, $N=10{,}000$, Laplace innovations, and a terminal threshold label; graph/coupling density is $0.2$, $0.2$, and $0.3$.  The families expose different transition structures, but the comparisons do not identify mechanism family or contraction as a cause of observed differences.  Generator cards are in Appendix~\ref{app:generators}.
LinearSCM-T is contractive by construction.  NlinearSCM-T is bounded but its
spectral cap does not itself prove branch-level contraction.  SpringSCM-T is
undamped and near-conservative.  This variation challenges the same audit
under different dynamics; it is not a controlled ablation of contraction.

\subsection{Tiers and what each can support}
\label{sec:setup-tiers}

Only the synthetic tier has the written $G$, $F$, noise, and label functional needed for exact replay.  Real data could test transfer of an observed pattern but cannot validate these mechanism-relative metrics; no real-data result is used here.

\subsection{Methods and controls}
\label{sec:setup-methods}

One LSTM per family is held fixed across methods and shift evaluation (test accuracy $0.991$, $0.999$, and $0.994$).  The baselines are Wachter \citep{wachter2017counterfactual}, COMTE \citep{ates2021comte}, CONFETTI \citep{paredescetina2025confetti}, and M-CELS \citep{li2024mcels}; none receives $G$ or $F$.  TSCausalCF \citep{bahri2025causalfeasibility} receives both as a residual-penalty foil.  PearlSCMRecourse and NoiselessSCMRecourse are structural controls (Appendix~\ref{app:controls}).
The controls optimise one action and let either the Pearl recursion or the
noiseless skeleton generate the suffix.  They test the scoring semantics; they
are not competing post-hoc explainers.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol and reporting scope}
\label{sec:setup-protocol}

Each configuration uses seed $42$.  We select the first $100$ held-out instances predicted outside the target class and evaluate all seven methods on the same factuals.  The shift preserves graph, mechanism, seed, and classifier while replacing Laplace with uniform innovations.  Tables are descriptive point estimates: they demonstrate disagreement between measurement systems, not stable rankings across SCMs or classifier fits.  We report denominators and abstentions; construction diagnostics remain outside the manuscript.
Shift-VR is shifted/base validity when base validity is at least $0.30$; below
that threshold both rates are retained and the unstable ratio is suppressed.
No smoke configuration or dataset-level diagnostic enters a paper table.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The same proposals receive a conventional model-relative reading and a structural replay; the contrast demonstrates a measurement gap rather than an inferential ranking.

\label{sec:res-conventional}

baseline validity is at least $0.50$ in $11/12$ cells and at least $0.80$ in

\paragraph{Controls anchor the intervention semantics.}
\label{sec:res-controls}

NoiselessSCMRecourse has rollout/Pearl CF-faith $1/0$ in every family;
PearlSCMRecourse has $0/1$, $D=1$, and $\dototal=0.29,0.00,0.02$.
Reproducing the noiseless continuation under Pearl semantics instead requires
$D=75.0,59.7,73.0$.  The controls recover their construction and show why the
intervention reading must be named.  In particular, the noiseless control is
not a failed causal method when its Pearl score is zero: it intentionally
answers a different counterfactual question.  Conversely, the Pearl control's
unit do-complexity and perfect Pearl faithfulness show that the audit does not
penalise every long changed suffix merely because many values differ from the
factual.  It distinguishes values generated by propagation from values that
must be imposed.

\paragraph{Mechanism consistency is largely absent.}
\label{sec:res-audit}

For the four baselines, hard rollout CF-faith is zero in all $12$ cells and
hard Pearl CF-faith is zero in nine.  Only M-CELS has nonzero Pearl fractions
($0.075,0.24,0.40$), so favourable validity and sparsity do not imply an
SCM-consistent suffix.  This is the first reversal of the conventional account:
the methods often find inputs the classifier accepts, but almost none of those
inputs is the continuation generated by one declared action and the factual
innovations.  CF-faith is an admission check rather than a ranking: zero marks
a mismatch with the named semantics, while a nonzero fraction still requires
$D$ and outcome replay to determine what was admitted.

\paragraph{Replay locates the disagreement.}
\label{sec:res-decomposition}

$A>C$ in every baseline cell ($\dototal=0.25$--$1.00$).  Moreover,
$\dooutcome=0$ in $10/12$ cells and is only $-0.01,0.02$ in the others;
$\dotraj$ therefore accounts for nearly all disagreement.  The observed gap
is between proposal and structural replay, not classifier and label rule on
the replay.  This localization matters.  It means that the headline failure
cannot generally be repaired by recalibrating the fixed LSTM on trajectories
that the world actually produces: on those replays, the classifier and the
known label rule nearly always agree.  Instead, the classifier is evaluated on
a proposed trajectory that the corresponding intervention does not realise.
The audit therefore changes the empirical question from ``did the edited input
flip the model?'' to ``did the stated action generate the edited input, and did
its propagated consequences retain the target outcome?''

\paragraph{Do-complexity reveals trajectory rewrites.}
\label{sec:res-complexity}

COMTE and scorable CONFETTI proposals have $D=100$; Wachter has $98.3,100,100$.
They are near-complete schedules under Pearl semantics.  M-CELS has
$D=6.7,26.4,55.3$, but these values change $6.1$--$16.5\times$ under the
registered threshold perturbation.  Only the ordinal ``less complete''
comparison is supported, and its claimed rate still exceeds replay in every
family.  This prevents a misleading interpretation of low replay error under a
full schedule: if every timestep is declared an action, reproducing the
proposal says little about causal propagation.  COMTE and CONFETTI illustrate
this vacuity directly.  M-CELS intervenes less extensively, but the threshold
sensitivity prevents treating its exact $D$ values as stable measurements.

\paragraph{Residual regularisation is not structural derivation.}
\label{sec:res-foil}

TSCausalCF has $D=100$ and zero Pearl CF-faith in all families.  On LinearSCM-T
it has validity $1$, rollout faith $0.55$, but replay rate $0$; on NlinearSCM-T
and SpringSCM-T, $\dototal=-0.36,0.01$.  Thus mechanism access through a local
residual is not equivalent to explicit structural derivation.  The nonlinear
negative disagreement also cautions against interpreting the smallest absolute
$\dototal$ as the best explanation: its proposed target-class rate is zero,
and the method is not solving the same successful-recourse problem as COMTE.
The components and the conventional validity must remain visible together.

\paragraph{Timing and populations qualify comparisons.}
\label{sec:res-horizon}

All labels are at $t_{\mathrm{label}}=99$.  COMTE, CONFETTI, and TSCausalCF
start nonempty proposals at $t_0=0$; Wachter is at $0$ except for a few Linear
cases up to $18$.  M-CELS median label distances are $6,67.5,95$; control
medians are $74,49,74$ (noiseless) and $49,49,74$ (Pearl).  These differences
qualify rather than explain performance.  Population also matters: Linear
M-CELS has validity $.50$ but $A=.94$ on $53$ scorable proposals; CONFETTI has
$56,88,64$ scorable, and Nlinear PearlSCMRecourse has $87$.

\paragraph{Joint interpretation.}
Read jointly, conventional validity and shift retention establish that the
baselines often achieve their model-relative objectives, while the controls
show that the audit recognises structural continuations under matching
semantics.  Baseline proposals nevertheless rarely pass mechanism consistency,
and their target-class rates fall after replay.  The decomposition assigns
almost all of that fall to the proposed trajectory, while $D$ shows that a
low full-schedule error would often require declaring nearly every timestep
an intervention.  CF-faith, $D$, and $A/B/C$ are therefore complementary:
they test what follows the mechanism, what must be imposed, and what outcome
the replay preserves.  Conventional metrics remain useful, but they do not
identify the causal object implied when an edited trajectory is presented as
recourse.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Conventional validity is a statement about a classifier, not evidence that an
intervention would produce the proposed trajectory or outcome.  A known
additive-noise temporal SCM makes that distinction measurable: exact replay
separates mechanism consistency, intervention size, trajectory disagreement,
and classifier--world disagreement.  In the three reported benchmark
realizations, conventional metrics frequently remain favourable while the
standard baselines have little hard CF-faith, often require large intervention
schedules, and claim target-class rates above those obtained by structural
replay.  The $A/B/C$ decomposition locates nearly all of this observed gap at
the trajectory level.

These synthetic results establish a measurement failure that model-relative
metrics cannot detect; they do not establish method rankings or real-world
causal validity.  A temporal counterfactual evaluation should therefore report
conventional metrics together with both CF-faith semantics, do-complexity
$D$, $(\dotraj,\dooutcome,\dototal)$, the audit population, and
$t_{\mathrm{label}}-t_0$.  Progress under this contract means proposals that
are mechanism-consistent, require restrained intervention schedules, and have
small $\lvert\dototal\rvert$ at a stated and feasible label distance.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Exactness is conditional on the written $G,F$ being the additive-noise
data-generating SCM, positive lags, recoverable factual noise, and no unmeasured
confounding; the presets also fix $L=1$.  A discovered, misspecified, or
non-additive mechanism changes the estimand.  The audit also depends on the
intervention reading and changed-coordinate tolerance, so CF-faith, $D$, and
$\Delta$ must be interpreted jointly.  M-CELS's threshold sensitivity supports
only an ordinal schedule comparison.

All labels are terminal, so these experiments do not test an interior
discriminative site; differing empirical $t_0$ also prevents attributing
method gaps to label distance alone.  Finally, the point estimates do not
quantify variation across independently generated SCMs or classifier fits.
They demonstrate that measurement systems can disagree, not stable rankings,
family-level causes, or real-world validity.  We propose an evaluation
instrument, not a counterfactual generator.

The intervention schedule is reconstructed from changed coordinates using a
$10^{-3}$ tolerance.  That rule is explicit and shared across metrics, but it
is not uniquely mandated by causal semantics.  A method could regard a dense
optimised trajectory as one high-level action, while the benchmark can only
audit actions expressible in its observed variables and structural equations.
Our endpoint readings therefore diagnose the cost of two transparent
interpretations; they do not settle which action language is appropriate for a
deployment domain.  The strong threshold sensitivity for M-CELS shows why
this choice must be reported rather than hidden.

Finally, all three families are synthetic, additive-noise, fully observed, and
use the same terminal threshold label.  This enables exact unit-level replay
but excludes latent confounding, measurement error, learned mechanisms,
non-invertible noise, and labels that depend on interior or distributed
trajectory features.  The experiment also uses one generated benchmark and
one classifier fit per family.  Additional seeds would be required for
uncertainty intervals and method comparisons; real data with an estimated SCM
would conflate explanation error with mechanism-estimation error.  These
limitations narrow the positive claim to the audit's construct validity and
the observed conventional--causal disagreement in the reported realizations.

\section{Audit derivation and claim boundary}
\label{app:audit-details}

The oracle in Eq.~\eqref{eq:oracle-cf} is a unit-level counterfactual because
it reuses the innovations abducted from the factual trajectory.  Drawing new
innovations would instead estimate $P(\mathbf{X}\mid\dop(I))$.  Exactness
requires the recorded graph and mechanism to be the data-generating SCM,
positive-lag causes, no unmeasured confounding, additive innovations, and an
intervention that does not alter those innovations.  Relaxing additivity
requires an identified inverse noise model; replacing the written mechanism
with a discovered one makes discovery error part of the estimand.

For an atomic intervention at $t_0$, let
$\boldsymbol{\delta}_t=\bx_t^I-\bx_t$.  In the linear $L=1$ family,
$\boldsymbol{\delta}_t=A\boldsymbol{\delta}_{t-1}$ after the action.
Stationarity requires $\rho(A)<1$ \citep{lutkepohl2005}; Gelfand's formula
implies that for any $r\in(\rho(A),1)$ there is finite $K$ with
\begin{equation}
  \|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{t_{\mathrm{label}}}\|
  \leq K r^{\,t_{\mathrm{label}}-t_0}
      \|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{t_0}\|.
  \label{eq:horizon-decay}
\end{equation}
Contractive nonlinear transitions give the analogous product of local
Lipschitz factors.  Thus the relevant distance is to the label site.  If
$t_0>t_{\mathrm{label}}$, the action cannot affect the label; for terminal
labels, $T-1-t_0$ merely happens to equal that distance.

\section{Metric definitions and axis taxonomy}
\label{app:metrics}

The validity argument is in Section~\ref{sec:audit}.  This appendix records membership, populations, and the numerical conventions needed to reproduce a table.  Every reported quantity belongs to exactly one axis.  Axis~A scores the \emph{benchmark} (one number per generated dataset).  Axes~B and~C score \emph{methods}.  Presenting all three as parallel per-method columns is a category error.  A former concept-quality axis was deleted; there is no Axis~D.  The audit quantities $A$, $B$, $C$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:abc} are not Axis~A/B/C.

\paragraph{Changed-element predicate.}
A coordinate is changed when $\lvert\hat{x}_t^i-x_t^i\rvert>10^{-3}$.  That single threshold defines $t_0$ (the first changed timestep), CF-faith's retroactive gate, sparsity, and the action set $S_t$ inside $D$.  If no coordinate exceeds it, $t_0=T-1$ and the instance is CF-faith-degenerate.

\paragraph{Axis C, model-relative.}
Validity is the fraction of proposals with $h(\hat{\bx})=y^\star$.  It is reported with $t_{\mathrm{label}}$, $t_0$, and $t_{\mathrm{label}}-t_0$; without that distance it is not interpretable (Section~\ref{sec:audit}).  Proximity is $\lVert\hat{\bx}-\bx\rVert_p$ over the flattened $T\times k$ trajectory, $p\in\{1,2\}$.  Sparsity is the fraction of the $T\times k$ entries left \emph{unchanged} ($\lvert\Delta\rvert\le 10^{-3}$).  Higher means a sparser edit; $1$ means the proposal equals the factual.  This is the complement of an $L_0$ altered-fraction; we emit that complement as \texttt{frac\_altered} and do not treat the two as interchangeable.  Channel and timepoint variants are the fractions of channels (resp.\ timesteps) left entirely untouched.  OOD plausibility is the Isolation Forest decision score on the flattened trajectory (higher $=$ more in-distribution).  TRSI is implemented as a mechanism-free smoothness descriptor and is not reported here.

\paragraph{CF-faith, both semantics.}
Given $\hat{\bx}$ and $t_0$, CF-faith fails if any prefix coordinate is changed (retroactive edit).  Otherwise it compares the suffix with one of two continuations of $F$, never averaged.  Under \texttt{noiseless\_rollout} the reference is the deterministic skeleton (subsequent innovations set to zero).  Under \texttt{pearl\_delta} it is the abduction--action--prediction recursion of Eq.~\eqref{eq:oracle-cf}.  A proposal in a noisy SCM is not expected to be both.  The residual is the mean $L_1$ distance between the proposed suffix and the reference; the hard score is $1$ if that residual is below $10^{-3}$ and $0$ otherwise.  The soft score $\exp(-\mathrm{residual})$ is the graded companion.  This paper reports the hard fraction.  When $t_0\ge T-1$ both scores are NaN, not $1$: there is no suffix to test.  Aggregates use the scorable subset and are published with \texttt{frac\_degenerate} and \texttt{n\_cf\_faith\_scorable}.

\paragraph{Do-complexity and populations.}
$D$ is Eq.~\eqref{eq:do-complexity}, counted on timesteps, under Pearl semantics unless a noiseless reading is named.  $D=0$ is a no-action proposal; \texttt{frac\_vacuous} is that row of the same distribution.  Flip candidates are $\mathcal{C}_{y^\star}=\{n:h(\bx^{(n)})\neq y^\star\}$.  The scorable population $\mathcal{S}_{y^\star}$ further drops empty schedules.  No-action proposals abstain from Eq.~\eqref{eq:abc}; we report their fraction and the remaining denominator rather than scoring them as correct zero-effect interventions.  Because $\mathcal{S}_{y^\star}$ conditions on the model being outside the target class, the estimand is the sufficiency direction.  PN, PS, and their PNS combination are defined in the protocol and are outside this paper's scope.

\paragraph{Sign of $\Delta$.}
$\dototal=A-C$ is signed.  Positive means over-claimed efficacy (the explanation asserts a target-class outcome the world denies); negative means the classifier misses an effect present under the label rule $y_g$.  Distance from zero measures disagreement; the sign is retained for diagnosis.  $\dotraj$ and $\dooutcome$ locate the source and are not a second leaderboard.  A $\Delta$ of either kind is never cited without $D$ and the intervention reading.

\paragraph{Axis B.}
Shift-VR is validity retention on a held-out environment of the same SCM (frozen classifier): $\mathrm{validity}_{\mathrm{shift}}/\mathrm{validity}_{\mathrm{base}}$ when base validity is at least $0.30$.  It is reported in Table~\ref{tab:shift-results}; below that threshold, the base and shifted rates are shown but the unstable ratio is suppressed.  Input sensitivity is a Lipschitz constant of the explanation in the input, where exposed, and is not reported.

\paragraph{Axis A.}
Computed once per dataset against the written graph, not against any explainer.  SHD is the number of disagreeing lagged-edge decisions.  Lag accuracy is recall of true lagged edges; lagged-edge F1 is the precision-aware companion.  Graph AUC is edge-level ROC against ground truth (NaN if the score matrix is constant or the labels are degenerate).  Residual dependence is the mean $\lvert$Pearson $r\rvert$ of mechanism residuals on non-adjacent channels.  Graph-error is the signed difference of CF-faith against the true graph minus CF-faith against an inferred graph; it can be negative and is not scale-free across mechanism families.  These construction checks are retained in the experiment artifacts but are not manuscript results.  On SpringSCM-T the recorded graph omits the position-from-velocity self-coupling (Appendix~\ref{app:generators}), so those Axis~A numbers are not comparable to the VAR/MLP scores without that note.

\section{Generator specifications}
\label{app:generators}

The paper reports three families: LinearSCM-T, NlinearSCM-T, and SpringSCM-T.  Other generator scales exist in the code for plumbing and tests and are not reported.

All three families share the same outer protocol.  A lagged graph $G\in\{0,1\}^{k\times k\times L}$ is drawn with independent Bernoulli($s$) edges, except that lag-1 self-loops are forbidden.  Innovations are additive Laplace with scale $0.1$.  A burn-in of $100$ steps is discarded.  The binary label is a median split, over the generated population, of channel~$0$ at $t_{\mathrm{label}}=T-1$, and the resulting threshold is recorded with the audit artifacts.  Observation is the identity: nonlinear mixing $x=g(z)$ is out of scope, because it would make abduction partial.

\paragraph{LinearSCM-T.}
A stable VAR,
\begin{equation}
  x_t=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}A_\ell x_{t-\ell}+\varepsilon_t.
\end{equation}
Entries of each $A_\ell$ are drawn from $U(-0.5,0.5)$, masked by $G$, and rescaled so that $\rho(A_\ell)\le 0.9$.  With $L=1$ this is the contraction that Eq.~\eqref{eq:horizon-decay} uses.

\paragraph{NlinearSCM-T.}
The same $G$ and additive noise, with the linear map replaced by a per-node two-layer MLP
\begin{equation}
  x_t^i=\mathrm{decay}_i\,x_{t-1}^i+\mathrm{gain}\cdot\tanh\bigl(\mathrm{MLP}_i(\text{masked lags})\bigr)+\varepsilon_t^i.
\end{equation}
Registered hyperparameters are hidden width $16$, $\mathrm{gain}=1.5$, $\mathrm{decay}_i\sim U[0.1,0.4]$, spectral-norm cap $6.0$ on each weight matrix, and init-gain $3.0$.  Masked inputs are scaled by $1/\sqrt{\max(n_{\mathrm{parents}},1)}$.  Boundedness follows from $\mathrm{gain}\cdot\tanh$ and $\mathrm{decay}<1$; the cap of $6.0$ does not itself make the nonlinear branch Lipschitz-contractive, so dissipation is an empirical property of the family rather than a constructive one.  A randomly initialised two-layer net stays close to its own linearisation, so this family is a nonlinear \emph{parameterisation} of an additive-noise transition, not a strongly curved mechanism.

\paragraph{SpringSCM-T.}
An undamped second-order particle system, adopted as used by \citet{bahri2025causalfeasibility}.  Five particles are exposed as $k=10$ channels (position then velocity).  One symplectic-Euler step with spring constant $0.3$, $\Delta t=0.1$, and unit mass gives
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    a_t^i &= -\sum_j K_{ij}(x_t^i-x_t^j),\\
    v_{t+1}^i &= v_t^i+\Delta t\,a_t^i,\\
    x_{t+1}^i &= x_t^i+\Delta t\,v_t^i.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Additive innovations are then applied as in the other families.  $K$ is $0.3$ times a particle-level Bernoulli($0.3$) mask with no self-coupling; the last two particles have their incoming edges zeroed, so two exogenous particles exist by construction.  There is no damping term.  The recorded graph stores only the velocity-from-position couplings; the position-from-velocity self-coupling is treated as baseline structure and omitted, so Axis~A graph scores on this family are not comparable to the VAR/MLP scores without that note.  The label is the same channel-$0$ median threshold as the other families (first-particle position), not an energy functional.

\paragraph{Registered deviations.}
The original design space listed $k\in\{5,10,20\}$, $L\in\{1,3\}$, $s\in\{0.1,0.2,0.3\}$, $T\in\{50,100,200\}$, and a segment count.  The three families lock $k=10$, $L=1$, $T=100$, $N=10{,}000$. \section{Control methods}
\label{app:controls}

PearlSCMRecourse and NoiselessSCMRecourse are positive controls constructed by this work, not adapters of a published explainer.  Both receive the true mechanism $F$.  Both leave the factual prefix unchanged, optimise a single slice $\bx_{t_0}+\boldsymbol{\delta}$, and let $F$ produce the suffix.  A non-vacuous proposal therefore has $D=1$ by construction.  Candidate intervention times are $t_0/T\in\{0.25,0.5\}$.  The search minimises
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_{\mathrm{pred}}\,\mathrm{CE}\bigl(h(\hat{\bx}),\,y^\star\bigr)
  +\lambda_{\mathrm{prox}}\lVert\boldsymbol{\delta}\rVert_2^2
\end{equation}
with Adam over $\boldsymbol{\delta}\in\R^k$ (300 steps for NoiselessSCMRecourse, 500 for PearlSCMRecourse).  If that search does not produce a genuine above-tolerance flip, one prediction-only retry with $\lambda_{\mathrm{prox}}=0$ is allowed.  Registered weights are $\lambda_{\mathrm{pred}}=1$ for both controls, $\lambda_{\mathrm{prox}}=0.1$ for PearlSCMRecourse and $\lambda_{\mathrm{prox}}=0.5$ for NoiselessSCMRecourse.

\paragraph{PearlSCMRecourse.}
The suffix is the abduction--action--prediction recursion of Eq.~\eqref{eq:oracle-cf}:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{x}_t
  =
  \begin{cases}
    x_t, & t<t_0,\\
    x_{t_0}+\boldsymbol{\delta}, & t=t_0,\\
    F\bigl(\Pa_G(\hat{\bx}_t)\bigr)+\varepsilon_t, & t>t_0,
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $\varepsilon_t$ abducted from the factual instance as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:abduction}.  Hard CF-faith under \texttt{pearl\_delta} is therefore $1$ by construction, provided the instance is scorable ($t_0<T-1$).

\paragraph{NoiselessSCMRecourse.}
The same search, with all subsequent innovations set to zero:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{x}_t
  =
  \begin{cases}
    x_t, & t<t_0,\\
    x_{t_0}+\boldsymbol{\delta}, & t=t_0,\\
    F\bigl(\Pa_G(\hat{\bx}_t)\bigr), & t>t_0.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Hard CF-faith under \texttt{noiseless\_rollout} is $1$ by construction on scorable instances.  The two controls are not interchangeable: a noiseless skeleton is not the unit-level Pearl counterfactual, and we never average the two CF-faith semantics.
